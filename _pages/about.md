  <h2>Mission &amp; Scientific Vision</h2>
  <p class="lead-text">
    The Human-AI Interaction Lab (HAIL) and the Interaction Lab at Nanyang Technological University are directed by Prof. Kwan Min Lee, Korea Foundation Endowed Chair Professor at the Wee Kim Wee School of Communication and Information (WKWSCI) and the Asian Communication Research Centre (ACRC).
  </p>
  <p>
    Our laboratory explores to answer one central question: <strong>what happens in the human mind, behaviour and society when we interact with AI (or AI agents) that increasingly look, act, and think like us?</strong>
  </p>
  <p>
    Drawing on cognitive neuroscience, psychology, artificial intelligence, and communication, we study how people perceive, trust, and interact with AI agents, robots, avatars, and autonomous technologies in physical and virtual environments.
  </p>

  <h2>Theoretical &amp; Methodological Approach</h2>
  <p>
    Human-AI interaction requires rigorous empirical inquiry that goes beyond technical benchmark evaluations. Our research methodology integrates:
  </p>
  <ul class="clean-bullet-list">
    <li><strong>Cognitive &amp; Neural Measurement:</strong> Utilizing neurophysiological tools (such as fNIRS and EEG) alongside psychophysiological metrics to assess attention, cognitive load, and neural correlates of social presence.</li>
    <li><strong>Behavioral Experiments:</strong> Controlled lab-based and field-based experiments evaluating human responses to embodied robots, conversational agents, and autonomous vehicles.</li>
    <li><strong>Computational &amp; Agentic Simulations:</strong> Constructing synthetic panels and artificial societies simulating multi-agent social interactions to model complex cultural and behavioral dynamics.</li>
    <li><strong>Psychological Frameworks:</strong> Grounded in theories of social presence, the Computers Are Social Actors (CASA) paradigm, anthropomorphism, psychological ownership, and trust calibration.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Opportunities &amp; Joining the Lab</h2>
  <p>
    The lab welcomes motivated doctoral scholars, postdoctoral fellows, and student research assistants with backgrounds in Communication, Psychology, Cognitive Science, Computer Science, or Human-Computer Interaction (HCI).
  </p>
  <p>
    Researchers in our laboratory conduct empirical experiments, contribute to interdisciplinary investigations across NTU schools and international partners, and translate academic findings to industry and policy stakeholders.
